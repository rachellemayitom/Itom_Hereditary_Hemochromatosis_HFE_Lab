# Results Summary

## 1. Wild-Type HFE

The reference HFE transcript used was NM_000410.4, with the corresponding reference protein accession NP_000401.1. The wild-type coding sequence was 1,047 nucleotides long and produced a predicted protein of 348 amino acids before the stop codon.

## 2. Documented C282Y Mutation

The documented disease-associated variant analyzed was NM_000410.4(HFE):c.845G>A (p.Cys282Tyr), also known as C282Y. This is a missense mutation that changes the amino acid cysteine (Cys) at position 282 to tyrosine (Tyr). The mutation does not cause a frameshift or change the overall protein length.

## 3. WT and Mutant Protein Comparison

Alignment of the wild-type and C282Y protein sequences showed that the sequences were identical except for the amino acid substitution at position 282. The mutant protein retained the same overall length as the wild-type protein.

## 4. Artificial Frameshift Mutation

An artificial one-nucleotide deletion was introduced near the beginning of the HFE coding sequence. Because the deletion was not a multiple of three nucleotides, it caused a frameshift. The altered reading frame produced premature stop codons and substantially changed the predicted protein sequence.

## 5. Overall Interpretation

The documented C282Y mutation produces a single amino acid substitution without changing the reading frame or protein length. In contrast, the artificial one-nucleotide deletion causes a frameshift and premature termination, resulting in a substantially altered predicted protein product. These results demonstrate how different types of DNA sequence changes can produce different effects on protein structure and potentially contribute to disease phenotypes.
